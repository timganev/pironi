# weekly-reset

description: The recurring look back over a week and forward over the next, and what such a pass reliably misses.
triggers: weekly, fortnight, retrospective, retro, standup, ретроспекция, седмица, седмичен, седмичния, седмично, изминалата, следващата, предстоящи, застояли, забуксували, изостава, изостават, буксува, натовареност, капацитет

Where the calendar, the mail and the tasks live is a separate question — on Windows that is
`windows-outlook-teams`. This is about what a week's worth of them adds up to.

What to produce is the user's call. Ask if it is not obvious. What follows is what tends to be
true about a weekly pass, not a shape to force the answer into.

## Two directions, and the backwards one is the one that gets skipped

Forwards is easy and nearly worthless on its own: next week's calendar is already visible to the
person asking. The value is backwards — what was committed to, what moved, and what quietly did
not. A review that only plans is a diary being read aloud.

Both need a stated window. "This week" is ambiguous every Monday and every Friday, and a review
whose boundaries are not said is a review nobody can check.

## Where the week's evidence actually is

Four sources, and any three of them make a confident wrong picture:

- **The calendar** says where the time went, and lies about the meetings that were cancelled but
  not removed, and about the ones that ran over.
- **Sent mail** is the best record of what was actually promised. It is also the one people forget
  to look at, because it does not feel like a task list.
- **The task or ticket system** says what was meant to happen. Status fields go stale first.
- **Notes** hold the reasoning, and are where a decision that never became a ticket survives.

Say which of these were read. A summary drawn from one source, presented as the week, is the
common failure and it looks exactly like a thorough one.

## Stalled is a shape, not a status

Nothing marks itself as stuck. What stalling looks like:

- an item whose last movement is older than its stated cadence
- a thread where the user's own message is the last one, days ago
- a task assigned to somebody who was on leave for the window
- a decision that has been "pending" across more than one review

The last is the most valuable and the easiest to lose, because each individual review sees it as
merely still open. Comparing against the previous review is what surfaces it — so a review that
does not know what the last one said can only find the first three.

## Capacity is arithmetic, and mostly ignored

Hours already committed against hours in the week is a number, and it is usually the answer to
"why did nothing move". Meetings, travel, and time zones come out of the same budget as the work.
A plan that does not subtract them is a wish.

Do not invent a working pattern. If it is not known how long the person's week is, say so rather
than assuming forty hours.

## Traps

- **Recurring meetings inflate everything.** Expanding a series counts one commitment as twelve.
  For "where did my time go" that is right; for "what did we decide" it is wrong.
- **A quiet week and an unread week look the same.** Nothing found in a source is a finding only
  if that source was actually reached. Say which ones failed.
- **Aggregating hides the one that matters.** "Six projects progressed" can conceal that the
  seventh, the one with the deadline, did not.
- **The previous review is context, not truth.** It recorded what was believed then.

## Before anything leaves the machine

Producing the review is local work. Rescheduling, sending it, or changing anything in a tracker is
an external effect the user approves, and the harness will ask.
